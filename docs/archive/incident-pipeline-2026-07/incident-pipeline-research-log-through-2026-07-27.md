# Incident Pipeline Product Contract and Architecture

Date consolidated: 2026-07-26

Status: authoritative. This document supersedes the July experiment notes in
[`archive/incident-pipeline-2026-07`](archive/incident-pipeline-2026-07/README.md).

## Current production state

- OT and RPI use the established legacy incident pipeline.
- Every replacement execution, persistence, and ownership flag is disabled.
- The verified-standalone production experiment was reverted.
- Its 34 fragmented incidents and call links were removed in one audited,
  operator-authorized transaction; the experiment ledger and operation audits
  remain intact.
- No replacement design is currently approved for production.

## Product definition

An incident is a representation of one real-world event, assembled from the
available radio observations about that event. A radio call or transmission is
evidence. It is not automatically an incident.

The pipeline must support:

- legitimate events described by one observation;
- events spread across many calls, systems, talkgroups, or time periods;
- later evidence that extends or corrects an event;
- uncertain evidence that is retained without being forced into an incident;
- grounded titles, summaries, locations, and call membership.

Publishing unmatched observations as incidents is prohibited. A one-call
incident is valid only when the evidence supports a self-contained real-world
event; it may not be created merely because no relationship was found.

## Operator decisions retained from the design interviews

These are settled requirements and should not be asked again unless the
operator changes them.

1. Incorrectly combining unrelated calls is the worst error. Missing a valid
   incident is the second-worst error. False splits are not an acceptable
   steady-state substitute for grouping.
2. An ordinary publication delay of about 10 minutes is acceptable, and a
   longer delay is acceptable when it materially reduces inference demand.
3. Staying current under ordinary load is required. An occasional bounded
   backlog is acceptable if the system catches up automatically.
4. The replacement must support OT, RPI, and future PizzaWave systems through
   the shared Paxan inference host. It cannot be sized as an OT-only pipeline.
5. Paxan and its resident Qwen 3.6 35B-A3B model are the deployment target now.
   Model research and additional hardware are follow-up options, not reasons to
   delay a viable Qwen deployment or begin endless bake-offs.
6. The current LM Studio configuration is intentional: 65,536-token context,
   parallelism 4, flash attention, and the other operator-tuned settings should
   not be changed as an incidental pipeline optimization.
7. Titles should be generated by the model from accepted evidence. They must be
   concise and grounded, not clipped transcript text or silent ASR repair.
8. A title may change when new evidence changes the event understanding. The UI
   should preserve continuity with an `Updated` badge and a tooltip or callout
   containing the previous title. Avoid repeated cosmetic title churn.
9. Location is a first-class incident outcome. The pipeline must combine the
   evidence for an event before deriving its location. It must never fragment an
   event merely because one transmission lacks a location. A genuinely unknown
   location remains explicit rather than fabricated.
10. A well-designed operator review experience may expose unresolved
    relationships, but unresolved observations must not flood the production
    incident list as provisional singleton incidents.
11. Do not introduce a separate gateway, proxy, or scheduling service solely to
    make this pipeline work. Use PizzaWave's existing service and shared model
    endpoint unless a later measured requirement justifies infrastructure.
12. Do not encode incident membership with address, phrase, category,
    talkgroup, radio system, quality label, regex, event taxonomy, compatibility
    table, or hand-tuned semantic score. Retrieval and embeddings narrow
    context; they are never proof of membership.

## Authority boundary

The application owns:

- observation and incident identifiers;
- source preservation and exact citations;
- bounded batching and retrieval;
- schema validation and complete row coverage;
- append-only proposals, decisions, and title history;
- idempotency, concurrency, transactions, and audited persistence;
- incident assembly from confirmed source evidence;
- feature flags, resource limits, and rollback.

The model owns open-world semantic proposals:

- whether evidence describes an operator-worthy event;
- which observations may describe the same real-world event;
- the evidence for and against each proposed relationship;
- uncertainty and unresolved questions;
- grounded incident title, summary, and location claims.

The model does not directly assign database membership or mutate incidents.
Application validation proves that cited evidence exists; it does not attempt to
decide what that evidence means.

## Evidence reconciliation

The July trials do not support the previously documented two-pass graph as the
production architecture. The following conclusions are binding. A later design
must cite new evidence before reversing one of them.

| Trial evidence | What it established | Binding consequence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Whole-window state bake-offs | Qwen exceeded the practical contract on the full corpus; Gemma and GLM also failed contract or completeness gates. | Do not ask a model to reconstruct an unbounded world state. |
| Pair proposer/critic corpus | The proposer recalled 1 of 3 same-event cases and preserved 0 of 2 unresolved cases; pair latency reached 77.9 seconds. | Do not use per-pair proposer/critic inference. |
| Typed-evidence transition replay | Removing model-generated observation identifiers improved contract validity, but Qwen still reached only 0.889 relationship precision and 0.727 recall. | Exact evidence references solve identity integrity, not semantic accuracy. |
| Microbatch constructors A through H | Exact-citation prompts improved structural validity, but the model still omitted threats, anchored on irrelevant candidates, promoted administrative traffic, and invented a collision grouping across unrelated calls. | Bounded whole-event proposals are useful review material, not proven automatic membership authority. |
| One-sided link shadow and Run W | A sparse positive-link design admitted unsupported relationships, including name-only and generic-validity links. | Retrieval, a valid citation, or a second model agreement is not relationship proof. |
| Full microbatch replay | Exhaustive evaluation required 59,804 pairs; bounded retrieval reduced demand but candidate recall remained unresolved. | Pair enumeration is neither the production semantic unit nor the completeness mechanism. |
| Verified-standalone production | The replacement created 34 mostly single-call fragments that did not represent operational incidents. | Never publish unmatched calls as the default fallback. |
| Two-pass graph runs C and D | Qwen omitted the relationship between two near-duplicate dispatches even after the prompt was reduced from 16,851 to 11,706 tokens and every pair was represented. | The sparse edge task failed semantically; do not tune its identifiers, positions, indices, or prompt again. |
| Shared-Paxan A/B/A capacity run | The replacement used materially more tokens and occupied more inference time than OT legacy while RPI also used Paxan. | Capacity must be evaluated for all systems together, after semantic correctness, with no OT-only sizing assumption. |

The experiments also established four limits on what application code can
prove:

- identifier, schema, citation, and provenance validation can reject malformed
  output but cannot prove that two calls concern the same event;
- a second request to the same model is correlated reconsideration, not an
  independent semantic authority;
- model confidence is not calibrated probability, so a `90%` or other numeric
  cutoff would be an invented policy rather than a safety guarantee;
- an exact quote proves which supplied source a statement came from, not that
  the model interpreted the quote correctly.

## Reconciled production path

There is no tested Qwen grouping design ready for automatic production
ownership today. The production requirement is nevertheless automatic event
creation and grouping. Operator review is an exception path for ambiguous
evidence, not the routine admission authority.

The final Qwen candidate is a bounded rolling event-hypothesis workflow. It is
intentionally narrower than V1 and does not pretend that deterministic code can
replace semantic judgment. Qwen becomes the semantic grouping authority only
after one representative replay and one production-shaped shadow show that the
result is good enough under the operator's ranked requirements below.

### 1. Durable evidence horizon

PizzaWave keeps every eligible observation in an append-only pending-evidence
ledger for a bounded horizon, initially 60 minutes. Processing uses 10-minute
windows, but an observation omitted from a proposal remains eligible in later
windows with newly arrived evidence until it is grouped, explicitly dismissed,
sent to operator review, or expires under an audited policy.

This retains V1's useful reconsideration behavior without treating age-out as a
semantic decision. Pending observations do not appear as incidents merely
because they are unmatched.

### 2. One semantic proposal per window

Qwen receives one bounded working set: new evidence, a fair rotation of still-
pending evidence, and a small retrieved set of active incident evidence. The
working set has a hard observation and token limit; the 60-minute ledger is not
dumped wholesale into the prompt. Retrieval decides only what can be
considered, never what belongs together. Qwen proposes complete incident
hypotheses in one generation.
Each hypothesis contains:

- a grounded event account;
- the exact transcript excerpts supporting membership;
- evidence against the grouping and unresolved questions;
- a proposed title, summary, and location, each tied to supplied evidence;
- an explicit disposition for evidence it considers non-incident or cannot yet
  place.

The model must not emit database identifiers, opaque tokens, row numbers,
array positions, pair numbers, or compact indices. Those mapping contracts have
failed repeatedly and are retired.

### 3. Exact-reference resolution, not semantic parsing

Application code resolves a model citation only when the returned excerpt is an
exact ordinal string occurrence in exactly one supplied transcript. It performs
no fuzzy matching, stemming, token classification, regex classification, event
taxonomy, or semantic inference. A quote found in zero sources or more than one
source is ambiguous and leaves that evidence pending.

After exact resolution, the application owns source identity, complete coverage,
idempotency, transactions, and audit history. It does not decide that the
resolved sources belong together. That semantic decision remains the model's
proposal and must earn authority through the acceptance review below.

### 4. Event hypotheses, not provisional incidents

Resolved proposals are stored as append-only event hypotheses. During
development and shadow operation they cannot write incidents. After the exact
candidate passes the acceptance review, a complete, valid hypothesis
may automatically create or update one incident. The model proposal supplies
the semantic decision; application code supplies identity, provenance,
transactions, idempotency, and auditability.

Ambiguous, incomplete, invalid, or conflicting hypotheses remain pending. Only
that bounded exception queue is offered for operator review. It must have a
configured workload ceiling and age escalation; it may not silently turn into a
requirement to approve every incident.

A legitimate single-call event is still supported, but it is evaluated in the
same whole-event proposal task as multi-call events. The first failure to group
it does not reject it; the evidence carries forward. This prevents the
standalone-first regression while avoiding V1's loss of useful calls that were
prematurely discarded.

### 5. No runtime verifier or confidence gate

The production candidate has one semantic generation per window. It does not
send the same evidence to Qwen again and label the second answer independent
verification. It does not persist on proposer/critic agreement and does not use
a model confidence threshold.

Repeatability is checked by replaying the same representative cases enough to
expose unstable grouping, not by demanding perfection or inventing probability
thresholds. Runtime safety comes from strict source resolution, fail-closed
writes, append-only auditing, bounded resource use, and operator review only for
the unresolved remainder. These controls do not prove semantics; the direct
comparison with legacy and the failed standalone replacement determines whether
Qwen is good enough to own the automatic decision.

### 6. Publication and evolution

After acceptance, one complete event hypothesis creates one incident containing
all of its uniquely resolved members. Later windows may automatically propose
and apply evidence additions or event-account revisions under the same passed
contract. Membership changes remain append-only and audited. Material title
changes use title history and the operator-facing `Updated` continuity badge.

An unresolved item never becomes a singleton merely to clear the queue. It is
reconsidered with later evidence and reaches operator review only when it is
ambiguous, nearing expiry, or otherwise cannot complete automatically.

### 7. Why this is not Incident V1

V1 allowed one model response to drive grouping through a resolver and then
through another learned evidence decision, while static semantic policies could
silently accept, reject, repair, or split the result. The reconciled path keeps
the useful idea that a model can describe a complete event, but removes its
direct write authority.

The differences are concrete:

- unresolved evidence persists and is reconsidered instead of being lost or
  immediately published;
- the model never returns application identifiers or positions;
- exact source resolution is structural only and cannot silently repair model
  output;
- no regex, taxonomy, similarity, confidence, or second Qwen pass decides
  membership;
- Qwen earns automatic grouping authority through representative replay and
  production shadow results, not through a runtime score or second opinion;
- every accepted membership and later change is append-only and auditable.

### 8. One implementation package

Do not start another architecture experiment. Implement and evaluate this as
one package:

1. Reuse the pending-source ledger, bounded batch intake, exact citation
   resolver, title history, audited writer, and rollback controls already in the
   repository.
2. Remove model-facing identifier, row, pair, and verifier contracts from the
   active path. Keep the old code disabled until cleanup is separately reviewed.
3. Add an event-hypothesis ledger state that cannot write incidents during
   replay or shadow and can write later only through the accepted, feature-
   controlled contract.
4. Complete the Inspector workflow for the ambiguous remainder: source-card
   review, combine, edit, accept, reject, defer, and title-history display.
   Operators interact with application-owned source cards, never model
   identifiers.
5. Add fair pending-evidence reconsideration and expiry telemetry so every
   eligible observation is either grouped, reviewed, deferred, or explicitly
   expired with a recorded reason.
6. Replay the representative corpus and report automatic incident capture,
   false merges, fragmentation, unresolved workload, source resolution, token
   use, latency, and direct comparison with legacy. Repeat representative cases
   only to detect instability; do not tune between repeats.
7. If it is good enough under the acceptance review below, run one OT
   non-mutating shadow and make the explicit cutover decision. If it fails,
   stop; do not create another Qwen architecture under a new name.

## Good-enough acceptance review

The numerical research thresholds frozen on July 19 predate the later product
interviews. They remain historical methodology, not current release policy.
The current decision is comparative and uses the operator's ranked priorities;
it does not require perfection.

The replacement is good enough to shadow and then canary when all of the
following are true on representative reviewed traffic:

- unrelated calls are combined less often than legacy, and no recurring or
  systematic false-merge pattern appears;
- legitimate incidents missed or rejected by legacy are recovered often enough
  to be a clear operational improvement;
- multi-call events normally appear as coherent incidents rather than the
  transmission-sized fragments created by the standalone experiment;
- legitimate single-call incidents can still publish when their evidence is
  self-contained;
- ambiguous evidence carries forward, and the exception queue is small enough
  that operators are not expected to babysit routine incident creation;
- titles, summaries, locations, and membership are traceable to supplied
  evidence, with no fabricated source references;
- malformed output, model failure, or ambiguous source mapping fails closed
  without losing the underlying observation;
- results are stable enough across repeated representative cases that an
  operator would not see the same evidence regrouped arbitrarily;
- every metric includes its counts and examples so a favorable average cannot
  hide a severe failure.

This review explicitly tolerates occasional model mistakes. It rejects a
design only when errors are severe, systematic, worse than legacy on the ranked
priorities, or operationally burdensome. The archived July 19 thresholds may be
reported for context but cannot veto a product result that satisfies these
later requirements.

Only one satisfactory representative replay permits an OT non-mutating shadow.
The shadow is for production-shaped correctness, bounded exception workload,
and combined capacity confirmation, not prompt development.

Required capacity results are:

- ordinary complete proposals arrive within the accepted approximately
  10-minute product window; a longer window is allowed only when it materially
  reduces demand and remains operationally current;
- combined OT, RPI, and measured headroom sustain at least 1.5 times observed
  p95 arrival demand;
- an allowed burst backlog drains automatically within 10 minutes after the
  burst;
- no second always-resident model or always-on second semantic generation is
  required;
- no model failure can write production data or block evidence ingestion.

A failure of this acceptance review stops progression. Do not compensate for a semantic
failure with thresholds, another verifier, narrower retrieval, or live prompt
tuning. Do not compensate for a capacity failure with a semantic redesign.

After acceptance, rollout order is OT non-mutating shadow, bounded OT canary,
OT automatic ownership, then RPI automatic ownership. Legacy remains the
production owner until the replacement is demonstrably current and each cutover
has an explicit no-backfill fence and rollback. Trunk Recorder is never
restarted as part of rollout.

## Retained implementation assets

Retain and reuse:

- current production legacy execution until replacement acceptance;
- queue currency, stale-work handling, health telemetry, and no-backfill fences;
- exact citation validation and source-provenance ownership;
- append-only constructor, verification, and persistence ledgers;
- audited, conflict-safe persistence and rollback gates;
- title-history and combined-incident UI primitives;
- transcript-free capacity tracing and fixed-window replay tools;
- the representative corpus methodology and reviewed failure examples.

The two-pass graph implementation may remain disabled as historical test
scaffolding until a separate cleanup removes it. Its model-generated pair and
row contracts are not retained design authority.

## Retired approaches

The following are historical evidence, not architecture candidates:

- V2/V3 semantic policy and resolver paths;
- regex or taxonomy expansion as incident authority;
- whole-window free-form reconstruction;
- exhaustive per-pair or per-observation inference;
- required learned transcript normalization;
- proposer/critic agreement treated as proof;
- a second request to the same model described as independent verification;
- model confidence cutoffs treated as calibrated admission policy;
- retrieval similarity treated as membership;
- publishing verified standalone observations before grouping;
- grouping already-persisted singleton incidents after the fact;
- model-generated database IDs, opaque tokens, pair IDs, row numbers, array
  positions, or compact indices used to map membership;
- sparse pair edges, exhaustive pair lists, or connected components used as the
  production semantic grouping task;
- direct group output expressed as row indices or positions;
- repeated model bake-offs without one agreed product acceptance review.

Historical details remain under
[`docs/archive/incident-pipeline-2026-07`](archive/incident-pipeline-2026-07/README.md)
for audit purposes. They must not be used as the current implementation plan.

## Two-pass graph live result (2026-07-26)

The implementation passed 694 local tests, but it failed the product grouping
review on OT and must not own production incidents.

Run `ot-two-pass-graph-shadow-20260726-c` processed 24 observations with two
successful model calls. The relationship pass used 16,851 prompt tokens plus
10 completion tokens in 54.8 seconds. The graph verifier used 6,229 prompt
tokens plus 1,763 completion tokens in 139.6 seconds. Row-specific structured
output constraints eliminated the earlier cross-boundary citation failures:
all 24 results validated and no production write occurred.

However, calls 1503648 and 1503650 were repeated dispatches for the same medical
incident at the same stated address with the same patient description and
symptoms. The relationship proposer returned no edge, while the verifier judged
both calls independently publishable with the same title. Production ownership
would therefore have created the exact transmission-sized duplicate incidents
this architecture is required to prevent.

Run `ot-two-pass-graph-replay-20260726-d` replayed the same non-mutating input
after replacing long opaque pair tokens with compact application-owned integer
indices, requiring every eligible pair to be inspected, and removing the
six-edge batch ceiling. This reduced the relationship request to 11,706 prompt
tokens and 29.7 seconds, with no model or validation failure, but the proposer
again returned no relationships. The issue is therefore not capacity, malformed
output, missing same-batch candidates, or citation validation. It is a semantic
recall failure in the sparse edge-proposal task presented to Qwen.

Both runs were stopped with canary persistence and production ownership off.
Incident and audit counts did not change. OT legacy execution is restored;
Trunk Recorder remained PID 1433135 with zero restarts.

### Release conclusion

The two-pass relationship graph is rejected as a production grouping
architecture. Its deterministic evidence, ledger, transaction, and rollback
boundaries remain useful, but its sparse edge proposer, row/pair mapping, and
same-model verifier do not.

Do not implement the previously proposed replacement that returns groups of row
indices. Positional and identifier mapping has already failed, and a second
Qwen pass would not supply independent semantic proof. The only authorized next
step is the automatic, single-pass, exact-reference event-hypothesis workflow
described in this document, evaluated once against the representative reviewed
corpus before any further OT execution. Operator review is reserved for its
bounded ambiguous remainder. Current Qwen output remains non-mutating until the
automatic result is good enough under the product criteria above.

## Rolling exact-evidence candidate result (2026-07-26)

The authorized single-pass candidate is implemented and passes the local
product-shaped replay gate for an OT non-mutating shadow. It is not yet
authorized for production persistence.

The active candidate has these concrete properties:

- Qwen selects complete supplied evidence records rather than model-facing
  identifiers, row numbers, indices, pair numbers, or hashes. Structured output
  restricts membership to those complete records, and application code resolves
  them by exact dictionary lookup. Duplicate records remain pending.
- One generation constructs complete events, legitimate standalone events, and
  grounded presentation text. There is no proposer/critic cycle, confidence
  threshold, relationship pass, or standalone-verifier pass.
- A request contains at most 20 new observations plus one evidence record from
  each of four active or carried-forward hypotheses. Low traffic waits up to
  600 seconds. Under higher traffic a full 20-observation batch may run sooner.
- Hidden pending hypotheses receive half of the candidate slots in least-
  considered, oldest-first order. Consideration counts are reconstructed from
  the append-only constructor ledger, so restart does not erase fair rotation.
- Omitted or materially unresolved evidence remains non-visible. A later
  complete proposal can attach new evidence directly to that pending hypothesis
  without a second semantic request.

The reviewed replay used the configured
`qwen/qwen3.6-35b-a3b@q8_0` instance through the existing local LM Studio/LMLink
endpoint. LM Studio reported the operator's existing 65,536-token context,
512-token evaluation and physical batches, flash attention, four parallel
slots, eight experts, and GPU KV-cache offload. The candidate did not change
those settings; its largest reviewed request used only 1,153 prompt tokens.

The broad nine-window pass used 11,023 total model tokens and 239.6 seconds of
serial request time. It had zero invalid source mappings and zero false merges.
It produced coherent Park Hill, Highway 95 motorcycle, suicide-attempt,
domestic/self-harm, Highway 193 medical, Mountain Lake medical, and Trisha Drive
events. It kept the different Brandon Avenue medical dispatch and Smallwood
lift assist separate instead of repeating the old false merges. Five reviewed
strong standalone incidents all published; four routine records/status calls
all remained pending after the operational-event boundary correction.

The exact Highway 95 reconstruction retained the four core dispatch and patient
observations and carried two noisy later observations forward. The Brandon
Avenue and Smallwood standalone dispatches also remained pending in their first
window. These are carry-forward outcomes, not rejections or fragment incidents.
The accepted product policy allows that bounded delay, but the live shadow must
measure the resulting pending age and exception volume.

Unchanged repeats preserved the critical decisions: Run W remained separated,
Trisha Drive remained separated from Smallwood, all five strong standalone
events published again, and routine records remained pending. The incomplete
Kinsor medical fragment was published in two runs and left pending in one; it
never contaminated Park Hill. That is acceptable ambiguity behavior, but it is
an explicit live-shadow watch item.

The focused and full .NET suites pass: 24 rolling/configuration tests and 707
repository tests, with zero failures. The replay CLI is retained under
`tools/IncidentRollingReplay` so the reviewed evidence and scoring path are
repeatable.

### Decision

**Pass for one OT-only, non-mutating production-shaped shadow. Do not cut over
production persistence yet.** The shadow is now a confirmation run, not another
prompt-development experiment. It must validate real pending carry-forward,
multi-call coherence, title/location presentation, request cadence, and combined
OT/RPI Paxan demand. If those results match replay, the next code step is to
route complete rolling hypotheses through the existing audited/idempotent writer
and perform the already-approved bounded OT cutover. If they do not, stop the
candidate rather than inventing another architecture.

## Rolling exact-evidence live result (2026-07-27)

The authorized production-shaped shadow did not match replay and the candidate
is rejected for automatic incident ownership.

Run `ot-rolling-exact-shadow-20260726-a` was fenced above call `1506522` and
remained non-persistent. Five Qwen requests processed 100 newly arrived
observations plus four reconsidered pending observations. All five requests
completed without model, schema, source-resolution, or recorder failures. They
used 11,229 prompt tokens, 3,556 completion tokens, and 187.563 seconds of model
time. The run produced 11 structurally valid event hypotheses, including six
multi-observation hypotheses. Production remained at 5,950 incidents, maximum
incident ID 7,400, and 55,833 incident-operation audits.

The first live request exposed an overly strict application gate: Qwen returned
material with unresolved follow-up details and the adapter suppressed every
event. Contract v4 corrected that without adding a second model request or a
semantic application rule. The next requests produced useful grouped events,
including a medical response, fireworks complaint, armed-person report, and an
injury-crash continuation.

Contract v5 then proved the durable reconsideration mechanism. Hidden pending
evidence was reopened as evidence rather than treated as a published incident;
published incidents remained protected merge boundaries. The first v5 window
reconsidered two old routine calls and correctly left them pending while adding
two new updates to one previously proposed injury crash. This structural work
passed all 708 repository tests.

The following v5 window failed the highest-priority semantic requirement. Qwen
grouped a real residential fire-alarm dispatch with two unrelated transmissions
and added an unsupported patient-refusal statement to the summary. It separately
created a purported vehicle pursuit from two excerpts that did not support the
returned title or account. Every selected evidence record resolved exactly; the
failure was Qwen's semantic membership and interpretation, not identifier
mapping, malformed output, retrieval failure, or application validation.

The shadow was stopped immediately. OT legacy execution was restored from the
timestamped configuration backup, all replacement execution/persistence/
ownership flags were disabled, and Trunk Recorder remained PID `1433135` with
zero restarts. RPI was not deployed or reconfigured.

### Release conclusion

**Fail. Do not add a writer or perform an OT canary for this candidate.** The
live false join repeats the systematic limitation already seen in the
microbatch trials: exact evidence references prove identity but cannot make the
current Qwen model interpret a mixed window correctly. A runtime score, a
second request to the same model, a static semantic rule, or another prompt and
retrieval variation would repeat rejected approaches rather than repair this
authority gap.

Legacy remains the production owner. Further automatic-grouping work requires
a materially different semantic model/runtime or an operator-approved change
to the product authority boundary. That is a new decision and must not be
smuggled into another unattended OT experiment under a new name.
